# AstraPlusCar HarmonyOS Phone Controller

English | [简体中文](README_zh.md)


Both the target and minimum supported version are HarmonyOS API 23. Connect the phone, vehicle and computer to the same WLAN, then enter the vehicle's HTTP address on the connection page. The real-vehicle integration address on 2026-09-30, also the current default, is `http://192.168.8.204:8080`. Use the vehicle's actual IP if it changes. Interfaces and deployment are documented in [Phone API](../AstraPlusCar-device/docs/PHONE_API.md).

## Interface and Controls

Connection status places the vehicle in the center with video and lidar branches on the left and voice/model branches on the right. Lines connect all four modules to the vehicle to indicate their dependency on it. Video and lidar appear online only when the vehicle is connected and the module is ready; voice appears online after recognition starts in voice mode, while model status is currently fixed offline. Home and connection pages share this compact component. The layout passed a HAP build, and the four connecting lines and labels were checked on both pages on a HarmonyOS phone. The vehicle was disconnected during those checks and no motion commands were sent.

Video defaults to bounded short MJPEG segments targeting 30 FPS. Requests use `/api/v1/camera/stream.mjpg?fps=30&frames=12`; each closes after 12 normally transmitted frames. A new segment starts early every 300 ms, with at most three requests in flight. Each segment is retained for at most 900 ms; data arriving more than 750 ms after its start is ignored. Each segment's `X-Frame-Sequence` rejects duplicate or regressing source frames. If one connection retransmits, later segments can deliver independently. Normal playback keeps receiving continuously instead of repeatedly fetching individual images. The server still supports continuous MJPEG and single-frame interfaces. Capture is 720p / 30 FPS, driving preview is 640×360 and screenshots remain 720p. The UI reports actual decode FPS; duplicate source frames do not refresh the watchdog. Preview keeps the screen awake. Before connecting, the app binds the local-network route and disables the HTTP proxy. More than 2 seconds without a new frame disconnects controls and sends stop. A previously streaming foreground connection retries once after 0.5 seconds; connection or recovery failure prompts manual reconnection. Motion and speed changes are disabled until a new image arrives, and old sessions, keys and motions are not restored. Manual disconnect, cancellation or backgrounding cancels retries; the vehicle's 600 ms watchdog remains effective. Status polls do not overlap, late responses cannot overwrite a newer connection, and the app reconnects after a vehicle restart.

Portrait Home shows connection status, 16:9 video, the control pad and a bottom “Stop / Emergency stop” button. Portrait, landscape and connection-page video share a lidar-ring overlay, replacing the separate strip below the video. Narrow views adjust its vertical position and keep left/right distance labels within the frame. Rings and meter values appear only for valid distances ≤0.50 m: an API reading of 100 mm appears as 0.10 m, red is ≤0.20 m and yellow is 0.20–0.50 m. Entering the orange warning range (0.20–0.50 m) triggers one light vibration; entering red (≤0.20 m) triggers one stronger vibration. The separate RadarHaptics definition uses soft/single/intensity 20 for orange and hard/single/intensity 65 for red. Ordinary button light/strong feedback uses soft/hard, single pulses, both intensity 50. The shared preset executor caches hardware/effect support and logs startup errors. Updates within one color do not repeat vibration; simultaneous warnings merge into one, prioritizing red. Rings disappear for farther ranges, absent echoes, unavailable lidar or stale data. The connection component still shows lidar availability. Missing readings are not treated as safe, and positive distances are not clamped to a 100 mm minimum. Distances are the nearest echoes within ±30° around 270°, 0° and 90° relative to the lidar installation zero, measured from the lidar center. Align zero with the vehicle front. Near-field missing echoes may reflect a hardware blind spot and require comparing physical distances with raw readings, rather than an app-only conversion.

The connection page is explicitly top-aligned and returns to the top when reopened. A vehicle-address field with Wi-Fi / IP / port guidance appears first, followed by status, video and lidar. The page retains raw front/left/right millimeter readings, latest-scan age, color thresholds and measurement notes for on-site checks. The phone polls status every 250 ms without overlapping requests within a connection. The current server publishes a recent-echo window about once per second; faster app polling reduces the wait for a new window but cannot increase hardware/server publication frequency. Scan age uses the vehicle's monotonic-clock age directly, avoiding errors from its wall clock.

The center of the control pad captures an image; tapping video also captures. A separate “Stop / Emergency stop” remains at the bottom. The four main directions are forward, left turn, backward and right turn; corners provide rotation and lateral movement in both directions. Right-side labels stay inside their touch regions. Only one held motion can be active: a second finger cannot trigger another direction, moving to another region stops before switching, and release/cancel stops immediately. The speed slider remains usable while holding motion. It sends the latest speed about every 120 ms using the current motion session and renewal ID, without requiring release. Late speed responses after release, emergency stop or direction changes cannot update a new action. Disconnect, backgrounding and orientation changes release the active action; the vehicle also enforces a 600 ms renewal watchdog.

Landscape hides the HdsNavigation title bar with an empty title position, so content fills the window without a title-height gap at the bottom. Voice / Connection / Gallery are three overlay buttons at the upper right. Connection and gallery remain accessible; gallery retains its own title bar and back button. The 16:9 video is centered with its original proportions (`ImageFit.Contain`), with symmetric horizontal or vertical letterboxing rather than one-sided borders or cropping. Joysticks, speed and lidar bands overlay the video. One 12 vp elliptical band shows front/right/left proximity, without a rear segment or extra bands for danger levels. Bands and distances appear only for valid values ≤0.50 m and disappear for farther, absent or unavailable readings. Yellow/red indicate warning/danger; opacity rises continuously from 20% to at most 50% as distance decreases, with transparent ends. Color/opacity transitions take 320 ms; entry/exit fades take 280 ms. The ring center is transparent and the overlay does not intercept touches. Portrait and connection video reuse it; connection still displays raw readings and scan age. In landscape, the left joystick controls forward/backward/turning, with stop in the center. Right joystick left/right rotate left/right, up/down translate left/right, and its center captures. The upper-left speed container is 32 vp high with about 25% background opacity; decode FPS appears below. Direction controls use about 33% idle and 53% pressed opacity; stop/capture backgrounds are about 60% and 53%, with readable labels. The bottom command box sizes naturally to its text and padding, centers between the pads and ellipsizes long text to preserve the central view. The joysticks are mutually exclusive. Label positions and widths are calculated from the current diameter during construction: only fixed ratios are passed to `@Builder`, which reads the diameter internally, so resizing updates instead of retaining initial dimensions. The left center button remains available for manual stop. The connection page scrolls in landscape.

Screenshots are saved on the vehicle first, then downloaded into the app-private gallery and retained across restarts. They enter the system album only after the user chooses Download and completes system authorization. The vehicle has no log-stream interface, so the app provides no console page.

## Building and Integration

Voice control was added on 2026-10-05. A microphone button in the portrait title bar and landscape upper-right overlays enters voice mode; tapping again or navigating back exits. A connected vehicle and ready video are required. First use requests microphone permission. Core Speech Kit performs offline Chinese recognition with `online: 1` and `recognitionMode: 0`; the system captures microphone audio, intermediate text is displayed, and only complete final commands execute. Native UI Design Kit double-edge streaming light indicates voice mode. Landscape hides both joysticks and manual speed, showing recognition/execution cards and independent capture/stop buttons; cards scroll on short screens. Portrait replaces the pad with a formatted voice card while retaining capture and the bottom stop control.

Commands cover forward/backward, left/right turns, rotation, lateral motion, stop/emergency stop, screenshot/photo, acceleration, deceleration, setting speed (for example, “速度五十”, speed fifty) and exiting voice mode. Local Chinese grammar recognizes synonyms: “直行”, “前行” and “往前走” mean forward; “倒车” means backward; “左拐/右拐” mean turning. Combined commands are supported: “速度100前进三秒再右转” becomes speed 100 → forward 3 seconds → right turn 2 seconds. The whole sentence is validated before sequential execution. Speed changes, motion starts and stops between steps all require acknowledgments; any failure cancels remaining steps. If recognition splits a sentence, clauses beginning “然后/接着/再” append to the active plan. A new independent direction replaces it; stop cancels the whole plan immediately. Plans allow at most 8 steps and 30 seconds of total motion; unrecognized or incomplete combinations are not executed. The voice card shows the original recognition, interpreted steps and progress.

Motion lasts 2 seconds by default. Commands such as “前进三秒” (forward three seconds) or “直行半秒” (forward half a second) set 0.5–10 seconds per action, followed by an automatic stop. Voice motion reuses the control session and 150 ms renewals, preserving the vehicle's 600 ms watchdog. Entering/exiting voice mode, voice stop and successful motion/speed/capture operations provide haptics. Emergency stop cancels the active recognition clause so a late final result cannot restart motion. Exit, opening Connection/Gallery, disconnect and backgrounding release recognition/motion and cancel the whole plan. Orientation changes stop the action and discard pending recognition while preserving voice mode; the user must issue a new command. Permission denial, startup timeout and recognition errors exit voice mode and send stop. Reconnection does not automatically reenter voice mode or resume actions.

Voice regression: `node tools/check-voice-control.cjs` exercises actual ArkTS methods with simulated recognition/network callbacks. Coverage includes synonyms, complete combination parsing, Chinese numerals and decimal durations, rejecting negated/incomplete combinations, sequential execution and acknowledged stops, appended recognition clauses, plan limits, cancellation on failure, late permission/engine cancellation, deduplication/session rotation, action deadlines, stopping during speed changes, isolation of late commands after emergency stop/back/rotation/disconnect/backgrounding, haptics and failure feedback. HAP builds and offline checks passed. Physical-device recognition, streaming-light display, perceived haptics and real vehicle motion remain unverified.
